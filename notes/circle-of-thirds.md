# Circle of Thirds: first representation contract

Harmonia's first view is an intentionally faithful study of the supplied
diagram, transposed to C major as Harmonia's default. This note separates what the reference visibly establishes
from choices that still need confirmation from its author/source.

## Directly observed

- The seven degrees of C major are C, D, E, F, G, A, B, with Roman numerals I, ii, iii, IV, V, vi, vii°.
- Circle-of-fifths order explains the placement, but is not rendered as an edge path. Each chord is an open two-edge chain of thirds; its closing fifth is implied by the shaded chord region, never drawn.
- The three major diatonic triads are C–E–G, F–A–C, and G–B–D.
- The three minor diatonic triads are D–F–A, E–G–B, and A–C–E; B–D–F is diminished.
- Selecting a root shades its open two-link chord path with a translucent, round-ended ribbon. Multiple chord selections reveal their overlap.
- The keyboard is always visible as a stable projection. Selected chords place function-colored dots on every occurrence of their pitch classes; shared tones visibly carry multiple dots. This represents octave-equivalent tones without choosing one arbitrary inversion.
- Selection is chord-content based, not degree-label based: a chord persists when a relative-major/minor change merely reinterprets it, and clears when a harmonic-minor alteration changes its pitch content.
- The asymmetric edge lengths are meaningful: major thirds (four semitones) receive a visibly larger geometric span than minor thirds (three semitones). Chord quality does not affect link strength. Harmonia deliberately exaggerates this difference for legibility; the exact source measurement remains unknown.
- Node hue encodes harmonic function: Stability (I, iii, vi), Change (ii, IV), and Tension (V, vii°). Shape encodes triad quality: square for major, circle for minor, and diamond for diminished.
- The selected major key changes note names but not this degree-level structure. Roman-numeral mode deliberately suppresses note names so the same object can be read abstractly.
- The control names each shared collection as a relative pair (for example, C major ↔ A minor). The Major / Relative minor switch changes the tonal centre within that one collection; it is not a separate encyclopedic key list.
- Natural minor keeps its relative major's pitch collection and therefore preserves its underlying geometry. It re-centres the reading on minor `i`; its `v` and `VII` are deliberately marked context-dependent rather than dominant, because they lack a raised leading tone.
- Harmonic minor is an explicit variation: it raises scale degree 7, turning `v` into `V` and `VII` into `vii°`. These become the tension family. `III+` is the resulting augmented triad and has its own hexagonal shape.

## Deliberately not asserted yet

- Whether color and node shape encode a stable musical rule or are illustrative styling.
- Whether the outer loop itself carries a direction, and precisely what it means.
- The source's exact metric: which interval direction determines each distance and which color names each edge class.
- How inversions, chromatic chords, or voice-leading belong in this view.
- Whether all major keys should preserve this exact geometry or whether the diagram is key-specific.

Those questions remain source-research work, not application behavior.
