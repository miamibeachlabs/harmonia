# Audio exploration

The first audio experiment is intentionally a simple sequence audition. It plays
the chords currently in view—whether chosen from a preset or selected freely—in
their displayed order.

Each triad has one canonical voicing: among all inversions inside the fixed
G3–D5 register, Harmonia selects the one closest to C4–E4–G4. It does not
depend on the preceding chord, so a chord always appears and sounds in the same
place.

Each played note is additive synthesis: a fundamental plus quiet octave and
fifth partials (frequency ratios 1:2:3). Its soft attack, decaying low-pass
filter, and very small generated room are an intentionally warm sketch sound,
not a claim to be a sampled piano.

Each chord occupies a four-beat bar. The voiced triad is articulated on every
beat while its root sustains one octave lower through the bar. The compact chord
glyph deliberately shows only the three canonical harmony notes; the bass is
rhythmic accompaniment, not a fourth chord tone. All glyphs use the same
G3–D5 window, centred on the canonical voicing region.

Possible later questions: voice-leading between selected chords, individual
chord audition, tempo, and a more piano-like timbre.
